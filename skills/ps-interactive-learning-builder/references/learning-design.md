# Learning Design Contract

Choose the format from the learner's goals, prerequisites, and likely retrieval behavior.

- Select a **structured course** for sequential prerequisites, practiced skill acquisition, exercises, and progress checks.
- Select an **explorable reference** for non-linear discovery, architecture exploration, comparisons, and just-in-time lookup.
- Select a **hybrid** when learners need a guided path plus optional deep dives, simulations, or reference views.

Write measurable objectives with observable outcomes. Make each objective map to relevant claims, a module, and at least one suitable interaction, exercise, or assessment. Require every interaction, exercise, or assessment to teach, practice, reveal, or test an objective; remove decorative interactions. Check assessment alignment explicitly: verify that assessment difficulty, feedback, and evidence match the stated objective and learner level.

Order modules by prerequisites and cognitive load. Expose contradictions and research gaps instead of smoothing them into certainty. Keep unsupported claims out of lessons. Use authoritative dated sources, and label confidence or uncertainty where the learner could otherwise mistake interpretation for fact.

## Financial controls

For financial content, record valuation and retrieval dates for all time-sensitive inputs. State currencies and units wherever values appear. Show explicit assumptions and formulas so learners can reproduce calculations. When projections are used, present base, upside, and downside scenarios and perform sensitivity analysis for material assumptions. Separate sourced facts from estimates and interpretations.

State a clear educational-not-personal-financial-advice boundary. Do not personalize investment, tax, legal, or risk recommendations. Stop publication of the affected claim when dated evidence, units, assumptions, or material source access cannot be verified.
